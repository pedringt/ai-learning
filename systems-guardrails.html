Meridian · safety first

Guardrails skip drafting before retrieval for high-risk categories

The system design itself enforces the "simplest reliable" principle: financial, security, and account-change tickets never reach the AI drafting stage. The guard check happens immediately after classification, so there's no paper trail of AI attempting work it shouldn't.

  • High-risk categories route directly to humans without AI attempting a draft—this is faster and safer than generating a draft and then filtering it.
  • Mandatory human review is retained for all AI drafts; selectivity comes post-pilot only after data proves accuracy is sufficient.
  • Ambiguous classification routes to "Needs Review" (human decides) rather than forcing certainty into the wrong bucket.

Harborstone · operator judgment

Human verification is the control layer for ambiguous extraction

The invoice extraction pilot kept human review as the control layer, not as an afterthought. When source documents are ambiguous or business rules are undefined, the AI handles what it can, and humans verify before submission.

  • The system doesn't try to achieve perfection in extraction; it aims for high recall (catch all rows) and human-checkable output (clear what's missing).
  • Business-rule exceptions and ambiguous line items are flagged for human judgment rather than forcing the model to make assumptions.
  • The permission surface is intentionally narrow: the model extracts, but submission authority stays with the employee.

Closing reflection

What transfers across the cases

The repeated skill is not choosing between AI and humans. It is reasoning about the specific decision at each step (classify, draft, verify, submit) and matching the right permission level to the risk at that step. The simplest system is often one that gives AI a narrow lane and keeps human judgment proportionate to uncertainty.
