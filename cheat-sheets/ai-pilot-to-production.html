AI Pilot to Production

What has to change before a promising demonstration becomes a dependable service.

The lifecycle

  • Discovery: identify the job, current workflow, baseline, users, affected teams, data permissions, and a non-AI alternative.
  • Pilot: constrain scope, define success and unacceptable failures, include realistic tasks, and measure time and human review.
  • Readiness: assign owners, pass quality/risk gates, verify data/security assumptions, and make the workflow supportable.
  • Rollout: start small, monitor, gather feedback, provide training and clear fallback, and expand only with evidence.
  • Operations: monitor behavior and cost; maintain evals, access, incident response, vendor contracts, and eventual retirement.

Release gates

  • Quality: representative evals plus critical edge cases; stop if severe failures exceed agreed tolerance.
  • Authority: tool scopes, approval, audit trails, and a reliable way to pause or roll back.
  • Reliability: timeouts, retries, duplicate requests, unavailable sources, and handoff recovery are tested.
  • People: named product and operational owners; support route, training, and escalation responsibilities.
  • Economics: plausible per-outcome cost including human review, not just model usage.

Who owns what?

  • Product owner: desired outcome, scope, success criteria, feedback prioritization.
  • Engineering/operations: instrumentation, deployments, incident response, and recovery controls.
  • Risk/security/data owners: data use, access, policy, auditability, retention.
  • Business lead: rollout, employee enablement, staffing and downstream workflow.

Worked example

  • A 20-person internal assistant pilot works well. Before rollout to 2,000, validate source permissions at scale, support volume, response-time limits, regional or language differences, cost under peak demand, and ownership when results are wrong.
  • A successful pilot proves feasibility in its tested conditions, not readiness for every context.

Questions to remember

  • What would block release? Who has authority to disable it?
  • What happens if the first pilot team stops maintaining it?
  • How will real-world failures become new regression cases?
