Learning Library / Product judgment

Buy / Boost / Build

Choose the lightest intervention that solves the real workflow problem. Custom AI is one option, not the default.

Core idea: Start with the business need and constraints. Then compare buying an existing product, boosting a working tool with AI, building something custom, running a limited pilot, or doing nothing yet.
01 / Decide

The decision path

1
Is the problem important enough?If pain is small, rare, or unmeasured, first improve the baseline or process.
2
Does an existing product solve most of the workflow?If yes, compare buying or configuring it before building custom software.
3
Is a good existing workflow missing one useful capability?If yes, consider boosting it with a bounded assistant, retrieval layer, automation, or integration.
4
Is custom work justified by differentiated needs or controls?Build only what genuinely needs to be custom.
5
Is uncertainty still too high?Run a small pilot or discovery spike before committing.
Two legitimate outcomes: Do nothing yet, or pilot first. A real problem may be a poor automation target; a good idea may be premature because the data, process, or adoption path is not ready.
02 / Compare

What each option is good at

OptionBest whenMain tradeoff
BUYThe workflow is common, time to value matters, and an existing product meets most requirements.Less control, vendor constraints, recurring cost, lock-in.
BOOSTUsers already work in a useful system and AI can remove a specific bottleneck without replacing it.Integration limits and inherited platform constraints.
BUILDWorkflow, data, controls, or differentiation justify owning the experience and system behavior.Ongoing maintenance, evals, observability, security, and product ownership.
03 / Evaluate

Comparison checklist

Use the same criteria for each option so the recommendation is about fit, not enthusiasm.

CriterionQuestions to ask
Workflow fitHow much of the actual work is covered, including exceptions and handoffs?
IntegrationCan it connect to the systems, identity, data, and actions that matter?
Data & privacyWhat data leaves the company? What about retention, training use, residency, access, and auditability?
ControlCan we enforce approval, permissions, fallback, and prohibited actions?
Observability & evalsCan we inspect failures, measure quality, and verify changes?
Time to valueHow quickly can users realize a meaningful improvement, beyond a demo?
Total costLicensing, implementation, model use, support, maintenance, and change management?
Lock-in & portabilityHow difficult would changing vendor, model, or architecture be?
AdoptionDoes it fit current work or require a new destination and behavior?
04 / Recommend

Recommendation format

RecommendBuy / Boost / Build / Pilot / Do nothing yet.
BecauseTwo or three evidence-backed reasons tied to fit, value, and risk.
Main tradeoffThe most important downside you're accepting.
What changes the callMissing evidence, threshold, or constraint that could flip the recommendation.

Common trap and useful PM move

Common trapComparing a polished vendor product to a hypothetical custom build as if both are equally known. Label assumptions and uncertainty.
Useful PM moveTest the leading option on a small real workflow before broad rollout. Measure effort, quality, exceptions, and operating burden.
Remember: The best option solves enough of the real problem with acceptable risk and ownership, not the one with the most AI.
