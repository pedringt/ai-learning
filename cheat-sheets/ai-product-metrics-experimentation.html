AI Product Metrics & Experimentation

Separate model quality from user outcomes and business impact.

Three different questions

  • AI quality: Did the system behave correctly? Examples: task success, groundedness, tool-call accuracy, severe-error rate.
  • User outcome: Did it make the job easier? Examples: completion, time to resolution, correction effort, trust calibration.
  • Business outcome: Did it create net value? Examples: cost per resolved case, retention, service capacity, avoided rework.
  • An eval score can support a launch decision, but by itself cannot establish that the feature caused business improvement.

Metrics worth distinguishing

  • Leading vs lagging: early signals (feature use, successful drafts) vs later results (resolution, renewal).
  • Primary vs guardrail: choose one main success outcome; watch costs, latency, accessibility, review burden, and severe failures.
  • Rate vs count: “500 successful tasks” means little without eligible tasks, attempts, exclusions, and the time window.
  • Segments: split results by task complexity, user group, language, source coverage, and high-risk cases.

Experiments and evidence

  • A/B test: randomly assign eligible users or tasks to new versus existing experience. Check assignment, spillover, sample size, and measurement quality.
  • Holdout: keep a representative group on the baseline to estimate incremental impact.
  • Before/after: useful when randomization is impractical, but seasonality, changing task mix, and other events can confound results.
  • Qualitative evidence: interviews and issue review explain why a metric moved but do not alone measure causal impact.

Worked example

  • A support assistant shows 90% acceptable draft quality. The team still compares assisted versus baseline cases on actual resolution, total agent time (including corrections), escalation, customer satisfaction, and per-case cost.
  • If resolution is unchanged and correction time rises, impressive draft quality has not proven product value.

Questions to remember

  • What is the baseline and denominator? What change would count as meaningful?
  • What severe outcome must never be traded for speed?
  • What alternative explanation could produce the same metric change?
