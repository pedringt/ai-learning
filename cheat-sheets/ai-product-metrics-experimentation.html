AI Product Metrics & Experimentation

Separate model quality from user outcome and business impact.

01 / table

Three levels of measurement

LevelQuestionExamples
AI qualityDid the system behave correctly?Task success, groundedness, severe failures
User outcomeDid it make the job easier?Resolution time, correction effort, completion
Business outcomeDid it create net value?Cost per resolved case, capacity, avoided rework
02 / cards

Metric distinctions

Primary vs guardrail

A main goal plus cost, latency, severe failures and review burden.

Leading vs lagging

Early use signals versus later outcomes.

Rate vs count

Always name the denominator and time window.

Segments

Task complexity, source coverage, risk and user group.

MethodStrength / caution

04 / note

Worked example

90% acceptable support drafts do not prove value. Compare resolution, corrections, customer satisfaction, escalation and all-in cost to baseline.
05 / list

Ask before claiming impact

  • What is the baseline and denominator?
  • What change counts as meaningful?
  • Which severe outcome must not trade for speed?
  • What else could explain the same movement?
REFERENCE DETAIL / RESTORED

When a quality score misleads

90% drafts, no proven value: A support assistant produces 90% acceptable drafts. Compare assisted and baseline cases on actual resolution, total agent effort including corrections, escalation, customer satisfaction and cost per case. If resolution is unchanged and correction time rises, the quality score has not established business value.

Causal evidence cautions

MethodGuardrail
A/B testRandomize eligible users or tasks; inspect assignment, contamination/spillover, sample size and measurement
HoldoutMaintain a representative baseline to estimate incremental impact
Before vs afterAllow for seasonality, case-mix changes and other concurrent interventions
Qualitative interviewsExplain mechanism and frustration, but not causal magnitude on their own
Metric definition: Always document baseline, denominator, eligibility, exclusions, segment, window, main success measure and guardrails. Watch severe failures, review burden, cost, latency and accessibility so faster output does not conceal worse outcomes.
PRACTICAL FRAMEWORK / AI PRODUCT WORK

Quality × value: decide what earns investment

Use separate evidence for AI task quality and real workflow value. The matrix is a decision aid, not permission to waive safety, privacy or compliance requirements.

AI qualityProduct valueLikely next decision
StrongStrongExpand carefully; monitor quality, cost and adoption by segment.
WeakStrongFix quality and constrain exposure; block launch or expansion for critical failures.
StrongWeakRevisit the job, workflow fit, discovery and adoption rather than optimizing eval scores.
WeakWeakStop or fundamentally redesign the proposed feature.
Non-negotiable: Strong usage never excuses critical safety failures; strong evals never prove a feature is needed.
