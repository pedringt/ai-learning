Severity, silent misses, and boundary decisions

Severity should drive release decisions

LevelMeaning
LowMinor inconvenience; easy to notice and correct
MediumMisleading or wasteful; generally detectable before material harm
HighCan materially distort product decisions or undermine trust
CriticalCan directly cause unsafe, irreversible or externally consequential action; treat as a stop condition unless explicitly resolved

Decision-boundary regression cases

EvidenceExpected behavior
Unknown valueKeep it unknown; never silently fill with zero
Explicit 0Use 0 only when the value is positively established
Tentative 10%Do not present as approved or authoritative
Approved 10%Represent as a concrete approved target, with evidence
Observed 8%, target unknownKeep observed performance separate from a target
Previously proposed target withdrawnReturn the target to unknown unless a replacement is established

Product-oriented failure measures

MeasureWhat it detects
Miss rateMaterial changes or expected actions the system silently failed to identify
Unsupported-claim rateInvented, exaggerated or poorly sourced claims
Uncertainty-loss ratePending or unknown information upgraded to certainty
Unnecessary-review rateAvoidable human workload from duplicate or low-value requests
Silent failures matter: A visible wrong answer is easier to notice than a consequential case the AI never flagged. Sample no-action outputs and ask humans whether the inaction was appropriate; track confirmed false negatives and their severity.
Ownership and troubleshooting: PMs set expected behavior, severity and release bars. Engineering implements repeatable runs and instrumentation; AI can propose cases and analyses; accountable humans approve expectations and acceptable risk. Trace evidence → context/retrieval → interpretation → deterministic validation → classification → action before blaming the prompt.
