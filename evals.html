Meridian — Eval Suite

Evidence across applied work

Meridian: the detailed suite below uses risk-based cases for classification, retrieval, safe fallback, grounding, escalation, draft quality, confidence, and review routing. Separate subscores help distinguish model behavior from retrieval or knowledge-system failures.

Harborstone: quality is evaluated at the field level rather than with one blended accuracy score. Consequential fields get a higher standard; service-date corrections are separated into extraction failures, ambiguous documents, undefined business rules, and document-quality problems. Abstaining or flagging ambiguity can be a successful output.

A small, risk-based v1 suite designed to test the behaviors that matter most before launch. The goal is diagnostic coverage, not an exhaustive repository of tiny tests.

Design principle: one realistic scenario can test multiple dimensions. I am prioritizing cases that reveal failure modes, tradeoffs, or missing system rules rather than creating one trivial test for every possible behavior.

1 · Ticket Classification

Meridian uses one primary category per ticket: Account & Access, Billing, Product How-To, Technical Issue, Integrations, or Other / Needs Review. The planned set includes five straightforward baseline cases plus three harder cases.

Representative edge case

Customer-selected category conflicts with the message

Input: Customer selects Account & Access, then asks: “Why was I billed for a higher tier?”

Expected: Billing.

Why it matters: the customer's form selection is a hint, not ground truth. The agent should classify from the actual ticket evidence.

Representative edge case

Multi-issue ticket

Input: “I’m unable to log in and access my billing information so I can upgrade to the higher tier and generate the advanced monthly reports.”

Expected: Account & Access.

Why it matters: discovery established a routing rule: classify by the first blocking issue that must be resolved before the customer can proceed.

Scoring: category selection = Pass/Fail. Confidence is scored separately. Add a severity/risk note when a classification error could create meaningful downstream effects.
Guardrail insight: critical safety rules should not depend entirely on classification being correct. A prohibited financial action should still trigger the financial-action guardrail even if the upstream category is wrong.

2 · Knowledge Retrieval

The retrieval set tests whether the system finds the right Meridian knowledge, falls back safely when adequate support is unavailable, and keeps the answer grounded in what was actually retrieved. Four v1 cases are designed.

Failure localization

Wrong-source temptation

Input: “My monthly report is missing the latest dashboard data.”

Expected source: KB-108 — Troubleshooting Dashboard Data.

Tempting but wrong: KB-101 — Generating and Scheduling Monthly Reports.

Why it matters: a plausible final answer is not enough. The trace should show which source was actually retrieved.

Changed my mind

Stale / conflicting sources

Input: “What steps do I take to set up my monthly report?”

Conflict: current KB-101 and stale KB-107 can both look authoritative.

Expected v1 behavior: if the agent has no reliable freshness/authority signal, do not choose arbitrarily; escalate for human review.

What changed: I first expected the agent to choose KB-101, then realized that expectation was unfair without system metadata or a rule that tells it KB-107 is stale. This is a knowledge-system dependency, not simply a “smarter model” problem.

Diagnostic scoring: Source Selection · Safe Fallback · Grounded Use · use N/A where a dimension does not apply.
Why separate subscores: “Source Selection = Fail / Grounded Use = Pass” tells a different story from a generic failure: the model may have faithfully used the wrong retrieved article. That gives future trace analysis a concrete place to start.

Next eval areas

Draft Response Quality

Factual correctness, policy adherence, unsupported claims, customer tone, and whether the draft actually answers the question.

Appropriate Human Escalation

Test defined escalation triggers while preserving an important distinction: informational billing questions may still be normal support; unauthorized financial actions are the hard-stop boundary.

Confidence & Review Routing

Test calibration and whether cases reach standard vs. low-confidence review. A poorly supported answer with high confidence is especially serious because it may receive less scrutiny.

Offline evals vs. production measurement

Pre-launch / offline

Classification, retrieval, escalation, grounding, draft quality, confidence, and review routing can be tested in controlled scenarios.

After launch

Customer follow-up and reopen rates, successful closure, human edit patterns, repeated pushback, and recurring rep corrections tied to the same KB article are better production signals. Those signals can later become regression cases or KB-maintenance work.
