CAPSTONE ANCHOR

Success Measurement Plan

Define success before launch. Every later artifact should trace back to this plan.

Meridian PM → CS handoff

Meridian Support Operations is a simulated mid-sized B2B SaaS company (~450 employees) selling workflow-automation software. Its 14-person support team includes 8 Tier 1 reps and 6 Tier 2 reps. Ticket volume has grown about 40% to roughly 2,200 tickets per month while first-response time has slipped from under 2 hours to over 8 hours and CSAT has fallen from 91% to 82%. Two enterprise customers have cited slow support as a renewal risk.

Business problem
Support capacity is not keeping pace with demand. Leadership wants to improve capacity before adding significant headcount.

Known baseline
Average handling time is 12.4 minutes. Ticket categories are self-reported and only directionally reliable.
Agreed pilot
An 8-week Tier 1 pilot for classifying tickets, retrieving approved knowledge, drafting common answers, and routing uncertain or high-risk cases.

Human boundary
All customer-facing drafts require review. No autonomous refunds, credits, billing disputes, password resets, account changes, or security-sensitive actions.
Full handoff details

Approximate ticket mix: 35% how-to/basic product questions; 20% password, login, or access issues; 15% billing questions; 10% bugs/technical issues; 10% billing disputes or refunds; 10% other.

Current environment: The help desk remains the system of record. Routing is manual and inconsistent. The internal knowledge base has about 180 articles; roughly 20% have not been reviewed in the last 12 months, and reps sometimes encounter conflicting or outdated guidance.

Business objective: Increase Tier 1 support capacity and reduce customer wait time without materially reducing answer quality or creating unacceptable financial, security, or customer-trust risk.

Your job starts here. The use case and initial scope have already been defined. Your job is to translate the handoff into measurable success: understand the workflow, propose success criteria, validate quality, enable users, monitor performance, troubleshoot failures, prove value, and recommend evidence-backed improvements.
Before launch — clarify with PM: (1) What outcome did we promise this pilot would test? (2) Which decisions are already locked versus still open? (3) What conditions would make us pause, narrow, or expand the pilot?
The filter: A main metric should measure a success criterion, explain why it is or isn't being met, or protect a guardrail.

WORKING KNOWLEDGE

Understand the Agent

Portable mental model: Trigger → input/context → instructions → model → knowledge/retrieval → tools → routing → human handoff → output → trace/metrics.
Claude Literacy

Become comfortable recognizing: Claude models → prompting/system instructions → context → retrieval/RAG → tool use → MCP → agent workflows → traces → evals. The goal is not API engineering; it is enough fluency to follow, test, explain, and troubleshoot a real implementation.

Think beyond the model: Real AI workflows are often hybrid systems: deterministic automation/RPA + retrieval + LLM reasoning + tools/APIs + human review. The best solution does not use AI for every step.

Know enough to explain

  • What triggered the run and what the agent knew.
  • What instructions governed behavior.
  • How company context was retrieved.
  • Which tools/actions were available and with what permissions.
  • Where a human reviewed/took over.
  • What the trace proves happened.

Hands-on

Practice prototype — not production ownership: Recreate a simplified version of the handed-off workflow: support request → classify → use context → draft response → assign confidence → human review.

Purpose: understand, test, explain, and troubleshoot the scoped workflow. In a real engagement, PM and engineering may own production configuration.

Artifact: a one-page flow diagram or annotated screenshot showing trigger to human handoff, key decisions, and data sources.

Launch Risk Check: What data enters the model? Is PII involved? Who can access it? What actions can the agent take? What requires human approval? Are there security, privacy, bias/fairness, or compliance concerns? What happens when the model or a tool fails — and who owns the incident?

DAY-1 READY

Evaluate & Troubleshoot

Define expected behavior → test → inspect → diagnose → quantify → act.

Build a practical eval suite

Case typeWhat it tests
Core how-toQuestions such as exporting a report or changing notification settings.
Access + ambiguityLogin or access questions, incomplete details, and conflicting context. The agent may explain approved self-service guidance but must not perform account actions.
BillingInvoice or plan questions that may be answered, distinguished from disputes and refunds.
Technical + failureProbable bugs, insufficient information, retrieval/tool failures, or Tier 2 escalation.
Human-required safetyPassword resets, refunds, disputes, and account/security changes. The agent may identify the request and explain the approved process, but the action must route to an authorized human or system.

Meridian artifact: a reusable CSV with fields such as Case ID, Case Type, Input, Expected Behavior, Expected Source/Tool, Pass Criteria, Actual Result, Pass/Fail, Severity, and Notes.

Download Meridian Eval Template

Evaluation

  • Normal, edge, ambiguous, missing-data, tool-failure, and high-risk cases.
  • Expected behavior + pass/fail criteria.
  • Trace inspection.
  • Task success, failure category, severity, guardrails.
  • Before/after comparisons.

Troubleshooting

  • Reproduce.
  • Validate scope + recent changes.
  • Find where the trace diverged.
  • Categorize: instructions, context, retrieval, tool, data, routing, permissions, model, user input.
  • Quantify impact against the Success Measurement Plan.
  • Work around safely + escalate with evidence.
Capstone: Build a 30-50 case eval suite, then investigate a simulated drop from 91% → 73% task success and 9% → 26% overrides.

Worked example: The next scenario is a different failure pattern from the aggregate 91% → 73% performance drop above.

When AI Goes Wrong — Trace Autopsy: Meridian retrieves an outdated policy and incorrectly tells customers they qualify for a 90% account credit. Human reviewers approve two drafts and the messages are sent before detection. Document containment, affected runs, where the trace first diverged, why review failed, root cause, severity, customer/business impact, stakeholder communication, remediation, new eval cases, and prevention.
Suggested answer — check your reasoning

Likely first divergence: retrieval returned an outdated policy that should not have been eligible for use. The model then treated that retrieved source as authoritative.

Severity: high, because the agent made a customer-facing financial commitment outside the approved policy and two messages were sent.

Immediate mitigation: pause or restrict the affected workflow, require human approval for credits/refunds, identify impacted runs/customers, and provide corrected guidance.

Why HITL failed: reviewers may have trusted an authoritative-looking source, lacked visible source dates, or developed approval fatigue. Human review is a guardrail, not magic.

Prevention: fix source freshness/metadata rules, remove or archive stale policy content, show source/date during review, train reps to verify policy-sensitive claims, add an eval for policy-date conflicts, and rerun the eval suite before controlled rollout.

RAG troubleshooting

Wrong source? → stale/conflicting sources? → metadata/filter issue? → relevant text split poorly? → too much/too little context? → correct context retrieved but ignored?

Which layer failed?

Input → retrieval/context → instructions/model → tool/API → business logic → permissions → human handoff → UI/integration. “The AI is broken” does not automatically mean the model failed.

Post-launch improvement loop

Failure/correction → capture → categorize → fix prompt/retrieval/tool/workflow/data → rerun evals → controlled rollout → HITL quality gate → scale → monitor ↺

HITL Quality Gate: Before broad release, validate a controlled sample or pilot with human review against predefined quality and safety thresholds. Scale only when the change passes; otherwise return it to the loop.

DAY-1 READY

Enable Users & Drive Adoption

A good agent can still fail if people don't understand it, trust it appropriately, or fit it into their workflow.

Teach the real workflow

  • What the agent is for — and not for.
  • How to give useful inputs and interpret outputs.
  • When to trust, check, or escalate.
  • What humans still own.
  • How to report problems with Run ID + expected vs. actual behavior.
Safe-use guide
Use normally: routine low-risk cases.
Check: ambiguity, conflicting sources, unusual states, low confidence.
Human required: defined high-risk actions.
Escalate: failed tools, missing data, contradictory sources, guardrail concerns.

Read adoption signals correctly

  • Low usage + strong quality → investigate awareness, training, trust, workflow fit, or resistance.
  • High usage + frequent corrections → likely quality/reliability issue.
  • Ask what users do instead of the agent — and why.
Capstone: Prepare 8 Tier 1 reps for launch with a 45-minute training agenda + quick-start + safe-use guide + examples + feedback instructions + confidence/friction survey. Cover which cases to use AI for, what stays human, how to review drafts, when to escalate to Tier 2, how to flag bad knowledge, and how to report a failed run.
DAY-1 DEEPEST

Measure & Drive Customer Success

Connect agent behavior → user behavior → business outcome → CS action.

Adoption

Eligible-user adoption, active users, repeat use, team usage.

Are people using it?
AI quality

Task success, groundedness, overrides, escalation + reason, retrieval/tool failures, latency, guardrail breaches.

Is it working safely?
Outcome

Deflection/autonomous resolution, FCR where relevant, time saved, throughput, SLA, cost per successful resolution, ROI.

Is it valuable?
AI Metric Library: Start with task success. Add only metrics that fit the use case: grounded/unsupported-claim rate, escalation + reason, human correction rate, retrieval/tool failure rate, latency, high-severity error rate, deflection/autonomous resolution, FCR, and cost per successful resolution.
Customer health example: 25% Adoption + 30% AI Quality + 25% Outcomes + 10% Engagement + 10% Operational Health. Severe guardrail breaches override the score.
Meridian business-impact exercise: Meridian receives about 2,200 tickets per month. If the team's directional estimate that 35% are basic how-to questions is correct, that is roughly 770 tickets per month in that category. Validate how many are actually eligible for the pilot before claiming time savings, capacity gains, or ROI.

Deliberate post-launch mysteries

Weeks 2–4
  • 8 reps were trained, but only 5 use the workflow consistently.
  • Overall task success looks healthy, but one ticket category performs badly.
  • Adoption rises while correction rate also increases.
Weeks 5–8
  • A stale-knowledge incident occurs despite human review.
  • Response time improves, but CSAT barely moves.
  • Leadership asks whether human review can be reduced.
Report the story: What happened → why it matters → what we learned → next action.

As Meridian matures, use this vocabulary to describe how much responsibility the agent takes on — and how reliability, permissions, guardrails, and customer trust need to increase with it.

AI maturity: what could come next?

StageWhat it means
AssistAgent drafts or summarizes; a human reviews and approves.
RecommendAgent identifies patterns or risk and suggests the next-best action.
ActAgent takes defined, approved actions autonomously within guardrails.
OrchestrateAgent coordinates across multiple systems and steps to complete a broader workflow.

CS lens: Each step increases potential value — and the need for stronger reliability, permissions, guardrails, monitoring, and customer trust. Expansion should be earned by evidence.

SUCCEED

AI CS-First 30 / 60 / 90

Primary lens: AI Customer Success. The goal is to become useful to customers quickly while building enough PM awareness to partner well with product and engineering.

30 days — Understand

Customer + users: Listen to customer calls, shadow users/support reps working with the agent, and map common friction points.

System: Explain the agent flow, follow a run through its trace, and learn where telemetry, evals, incidents, and success plans live.

Translate: Map what I practiced with Claude to the company's actual architecture, internal tools, prompts, evals, traces, customer workflows, and ownership boundaries.

Team: Learn CS, PM, engineering, support, and account ownership boundaries.

If I’m helping shape the role: Clarify what success means to my manager vs. the customer; what I own vs. do not own; who owns discovery, configuration, escalation, adoption, reporting, and expansion; where customers fall through the cracks; and which recurring process CS could establish or eventually own.

Proof: customer/agent map + friction log + glossary + success-plan summary.

60 days — Contribute

Enablement: Co-deliver training or run a user-feedback session and turn recurring friction into a useful FAQ/known-issues resource.

Operations: Investigate basic failures before escalation and contribute to evals.

Success: Prepare adoption + quality commentary and recommend one evidence-backed action.

Proof: health snapshot + feedback summary + troubleshooting case + metrics analysis.

90 days — Own

Customer: Own meaningful onboarding/routine communication and lead a customer health or success review.

Proactive CS: Monitor adoption, AI quality, outcomes, and risks; investigate or escalate with clean evidence.

Value: Track against the Success Measurement Plan, communicate business impact, and recommend the next improvement/use case.

Proof: customer success review + outcomes/ROI story + recommendation + next-quarter plan.

Automate Your Own Work: As I learn the role, look for repeatable work AI can assist with — call summaries, action items, customer briefs, health-review prep, feedback categorization, recurring-issue synthesis, training drafts, metric commentary, and follow-up drafts. Keep human judgment for customer strategy, sensitive communication, escalation, and decisions.
Habit: Understand it manually → document the workflow → identify repetitive steps → automate carefully → review the output → improve.
PM partnership: Understand how customer discovery becomes requirements, how requirements become an agent workflow, and how CS feeds adoption data, failures, eval evidence, and business outcomes back into product decisions. Recognize when engineering feasibility, security/privacy, cost, legal/compliance, or another specialist needs to be involved.
Build / Boost / Buy

Build: create a custom capability when differentiation or workflow fit matters.
Boost: improve an existing tool/workflow with AI.
Buy: use a mature product when the capability is commodity and speed matters.

CS lens: know which path the customer chose and why — it shapes expectations, ownership, and expansion.

AI economics

Understand the rough business equation: cost per successful resolution/run + human review cost vs. time saved, capacity gained, error reduction, or revenue impact. You do not need to be Finance — you do need to know when rising usage or model/tool costs threaten the business case.
