How I work with AI

Boundaries, feedback, and evidence.

I’m optimistic about where AI genuinely helps, but skeptical of claims that outrun the evidence. I use it selectively, with clear limits and correction paths, and keep people involved where judgment actually matters.

02AI Opportunity & Product JudgmentDecide whether AI belongs in the solution and what kind of intervention makes sense.AI vs rules · assistance vs automation · bots vs agents · build/configure/buy · feasibility · prioritization

Strategy use

A real problem does not automatically need AI. I want to compare AI with simpler options, make the tradeoffs visible, and use more autonomy only when it earns its added complexity.

Build / boost / buy
03AI Systems & Technical FluencyKnow enough about the system to scope, question, troubleshoot, and communicate well.models · context · retrieval/RAG · tools/MCP · bots & agents · memory/state · APIs · routing · cost/latency

Technical fluency use

I want enough technical fluency to understand what the model sees, how it gets information and tools, where state lives, and what the surrounding software controls. That helps me scope better and diagnose the right layer when something goes wrong.

Bot vs agent: I use bot as the broad term for AI set up to do a job. An agent is a more specific kind of bot that can choose steps or tools to pursue a goal. For product work, the useful mental model is model + instructions + tools + context/state + permissions + an action loop + stopping rules. The important questions are what the agent may decide, what it may touch, when it must stop or escalate, and how we evaluate whether it did the job well.

04Quality, Evals & ReliabilityKnow how to decide whether the system is good enough and where failures come from.eval design · deterministic tests · AI judges · tracing · grounding · safe failure · monitoring

Quality use

I want to define good behavior before launch, test normal and high-risk cases, and trace failures to the right layer. Reliability also means planning for bad model output, missing retrieval, broken tools, and other predictable failure modes.

05Governance, Risk & Human ControlDefine what the AI may do, what software must enforce, and what people must authorize.permissions · decision rights · human review · privacy/security · auditability · escalation · recovery · evidence bar

Governance use

For consequential work, I want decision rights to be obvious. AI can interpret, software can enforce rules, and people can authorize the decisions that need human judgment. Higher-impact automation should require stronger evidence and clearer recovery paths.

AI governance, safety & privacy
06AI UX & Workflow DesignDesign the human/AI workflow, not just an AI control inside an existing screen.division of labor · trust · uncertainty · review UX · approvals · creative variety · skill retention · hidden work

Workflow design use

I want users to understand what the AI is doing, what they still own, and how to correct or challenge it. Good AI UX should make review and recovery easier, not hide uncertainty or quietly push extra work onto people.

Human capability, creativity & AI

AI can help individuals produce stronger work while nudging a group toward more similar ideas. Design for useful assistance without making AI the first or only source of ideas: preserve room for independent thinking, meaningful review, and skill practice. In pilots, check output quality and speed alongside variety of ideas, reviewer effort, people’s ability to challenge suggestions, and whether saved time becomes useful capacity or simply more work. Treat long-term skill loss as a risk to monitor, not a proven outcome.

Human + AI workflow design
07Implementation, Adoption & ChangeTurn a technically sound idea into something an organization can actually use and sustain.pilots · rollout · stakeholder alignment · training · trust · resistance · ownership · support · role change

Adoption use

A working prototype only matters if people can actually use and sustain it. I want to treat adoption problems as signals about the product, workflow, ownership, or role change instead of assuming training will fix everything.

08Product Analytics, Measurement & Business ValueUnderstand how people use software, whether changes actually work, and what business value follows.success metrics · instrumentation · funnels/cohorts · experimentation · AI-assisted analysis · ROI

Measurement use

I want to connect AI quality to normal product outcomes. That means defining useful metrics, instrumenting the right behavior, checking what users actually do, and verifying AI-assisted analysis before using it to make decisions.

09AI Consulting & Client WorkMove from vague AI interest to a clear, realistic recommendation a client can act on.discovery conversations · readiness · expectation setting · simple explanations · recommendations · uncertainty

Client work use

Client work starts with the business problem, not an AI pitch. I want to explain tradeoffs plainly, surface missing evidence, set realistic expectations, and turn technical uncertainty into decisions a client can act on.

Evaluation as a client capability. I want to turn eval evidence into launch and scale recommendations, and help a client keep a quality loop running after launch: production failures become new eval cases, which feed regression testing and updated thresholds and decisions.

Debugging AI agents for customer-facing work
10AI Landscape & Professional EdgeKeep up with changes that materially affect product or client decisions without turning this into a news archive.model/platform changes · enterprise patterns · standards · power-user techniques · terminology · market shifts

Landscape use

I keep up with changes when they affect what is feasible, reliable, affordable, or practical to adopt. The goal is not to follow every launch. It is to know when new information should change a product recommendation.

What these products cannot answer on their own

Building a coherent system is only part of product work. In a team, the harder test is what happens when people misunderstand it, reject it, use it differently than expected, or force tradeoffs between competing needs. That is the next layer of product judgment I expect real organizational use to add.
