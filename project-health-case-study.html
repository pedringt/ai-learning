Project Health

I built a product-facing layer over deployment, AI-quality, analytics, and investigation signals so I could answer one question quickly: what actually needs a product decision?

Role: product framing, UX, QA, eval review Focus: AI product health and decision support Status: working portfolio product

The product is not a prettier engineering dashboard. Its job is to translate technical and AI-quality signals into what changed, why it matters, what cannot yet be known, and what a product person should do next.

The problem: plenty of telemetry, weak product context

My projects already had deployments, commits, eval results, analytics, runtime signals, and live products. The difficult part was deciding which signal mattered, whether it represented a real product problem, and what evidence I needed before asking for a fix.

Before

Signals lived in different tools

A failed deployment, a lower eval score, and a runtime warning each required a different place to investigate and a different interpretation.

Product need

Interpret the signal before acting

I wanted one place that could distinguish a release issue, an AI behavior failure, a monitoring gap, and a decision that still needed human judgment.

The Overview follows the way I actually triage a project in the morning: attention first, product context second, supporting evidence after that.

Five product decisions shaped it

01

No composite score

Technical health and product quality stay separate. One green number can hide the exact behavior that deserves review.

02

Failures before averages

AI eval cards lead with scenarios passed and high-impact failures. Percentages are supporting context, not the verdict.

03

Unknown is a real state

Monitoring gaps are visible instead of being silently interpreted as healthy.

04

Investigate is one mental model

If something is wrong, Investigate goes deeper. If things look healthy, it performs a bounded health pass instead of inventing a second workflow.

05

Keep the human decision visible

Decisions, changes, and experiments can be recorded alongside the next release or quality evidence that follows them.

The most important behavior is what happens after a red signal

Project Health does not assume a failed eval automatically means “fix the model.” I used State failures to force the dashboard to separate product behavior problems from eval-contract problems.

State example

Two failures that looked similar at the score level required different reasoning

The dashboard needed enough context to preserve uncertainty rather than jump from “failed check” to “engineering bug.”

Behavior contract
New authoritative evidence must supersede a stale pending proposal

A pending proposal is not authority against newer evidence from the responsible source. The fix belonged in product behavior, not in weakening the eval.

Grounding contract
Known outcome, unknown reason

If State knows that something happened but the record does not establish why, Ask should state the known outcome and preserve the reason as unknown instead of inventing a causal explanation.

What changed in Project Health

The quality view now explains Observed / Expected / Why it matters, investigations can target the failed scenario, and regression context is surfaced separately from the aggregate percentage.

Investigation is deliberately bounded

1
Start from the specific signal

Use the failure type or incident to choose the first evidence source instead of opening every connected tool.

2
Gather the smallest useful evidence set

Compare behavior with the product contract, release context, logs, issue history, or eval definition.

3
Return a handoff, not an autonomous fix

The investigation summarizes assessment, evidence, impact, uncertainty, and next checkpoint so the human still decides what changes.

4
Look for the next evidence

Activity links decisions and investigations to the subsequent release or quality result when one exists.

QA changed the product, not just the polish

Information design

Raw event streams became operating history

Repeated deployment failures are grouped into one incident episode, Activity is grouped by day, and filters separate releases, quality, investigations, and decisions.

Trust

Healthy and unknown cannot look the same

Failure-recovery, freshness, monitoring-gap, and cross-signal consistency tests help prevent stale or missing data from being presented as healthy.

What is actually tested

Known-bad regressionsImportant AI behavior failures become permanent deterministic scenarios instead of one-off bug reports.
Cross-signal consistencyThe dashboard checks for contradictions such as “healthy” while a high-impact issue is still open.
Failure recoveryUnavailable runtime, analytics, or platform signals degrade to unknown/watch rather than implying health.
Golden product pathA deterministic State smoke test covers Evidence → Review → human authorization → Current State update → History, including the rule that a proposal is not authoritative before approval.

What I did, and what AI helped with

My role
  • framed the product problem and decided which signals should influence product attention
  • defined the information hierarchy and decision-support model
  • reviewed AI failures and decided whether behavior or the eval contract should change
  • ran repeated QA passes and used findings to reshape the product
  • kept investigation bounded so AI could support judgment without taking authority
AI-assisted work
  • implementation and code iteration
  • test generation and regression coverage
  • evidence gathering across connected tools
  • draft investigation synthesis and handoff structure
  • copy and interface iteration under my direction

What I learned

A useful product-health tool does not need to know everything. It needs to make the boundary between evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and human decision-making obvious enough that the next action is better.
