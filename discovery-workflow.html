Meridian · deep case

Discovery changed the implementation plan

Rep discovery challenged assumptions in the original handoff and changed what the pilot should optimize for.

  • A quick first-touch workflow was not actually standard, so it stopped being treated as a dependable mechanism for improving first-response time.
  • A copy/paste pain point proved to be a display/configuration issue rather than a reason to add another AI integration.
  • Knowledge-base conflicts and stale content became source-quality problems to address rather than prompt problems to tune around.

Harborstone · transfer case

A broad automation request became several different problems

The initial ask—automate vendor invoice processing with AI—was decomposed before choosing a solution.

  • The workflow contained extraction, interpretation, verification, downstream entry, exceptions, and business-rule questions with different risk profiles.
  • Maximum automation was not the objective; the first pilot deliberately stopped at AI-assisted extraction and pre-population with employee verification.
  • Ambiguous source documents and undefined business rules were treated separately from model extraction errors.

Closing reflection

What transfers across the cases

The repeated skill is not “finding a use for AI.” It is learning enough about the real workflow to choose the right intervention, narrow scope when risk or evidence requires it, and revise the plan when discovery contradicts the initial story.
