Experiment 01 · Completed cycle

Repairing a protected-account false positive

4 / 4 expected routes after revision
Objective

Test whether protected requests escalate without blocking routine account-access troubleshooting.

Failure found

“I tried resetting my password” escalated because the rule matched protected verbs and fields without considering intent.

Change

Require explicit request language before triggering the protected-account escalation.

Result

The false positive drafted after revision; the explicit password-change request still escalated.

Learning: A guardrail must distinguish a request for protected action from a description of a past self-service attempt.

Draft quality — how I'd measure it without adding another AI system

Ruled out an AI judging the edit

The first instinct was a second AI comparing the draft to what the rep actually sent, deciding whether the edit was substantive. I ruled it out: it's a second probabilistic system sitting on top of the first, with its own miss rate, and "the AI decided" is a weak answer if a manager asks why an edit didn't count. Quality needs to trace back to a rule someone can explain in one sentence.

A fixed rule needs something to compare

Comparing "did the dollar amount change" requires two saved snapshots — the draft as generated, and the text actually sent — plus a way to find the dollar amount in ordinary prose. Plain pattern-matching (currency signs, date formats) does that without a second AI call; it will miss softer phrasing like "next Tuesday," but that gap is measurable, not assumed.

"Start over" needed its own state

A rep who discards a bad draft immediately and one who nudges a mostly-good draft through five rounds of edits can both end up near 100% text changed — but they mean different things about the draft. Diffing alone can't tell a fast "this was useless" moment apart from a slow "this needed real tuning" one, so start-over is tracked as a distinct third action alongside approve and approve-with-edits, not inferred from the diff.

Start dumb, prove the need for smart

Regex ships first, not a smarter extraction step, because the actual failure rate of the dumb version isn't known yet. The plan is to log cases where a rep flags an edit as substantive but the pattern-match didn't catch anything different — that gap becomes the evidence for whether a smarter method is worth the added AI surface area, instead of guessing upfront.

Five evaluation areas

1 · Ticket classification

Straightforward, ambiguous, customer-mislabeled, and multi-issue tickets. Wrong routing is scored by its operational consequence.

2 · Knowledge retrieval

Correct-source, no-source, wrong-source temptation, and stale/conflicting-source behavior. The system must not be graded on authority information it was never given.

3 · Draft quality

Grounding, factual and policy accuracy, completeness, tone, and whether the response answers the actual question.

4 · Appropriate escalation

Financial, security, and account-change cases are included to verify recognition, no-draft behavior, and human routing—not draft quality.

5 · Confidence and review routing

Ambiguity, missing information, conflicting evidence, and safe fallback. Confidence must be tied to observable evidence rather than model self-assurance.

Failure diagnosis

Failures should be classified before fixing them: taxonomy/routing, retrieval, source authority or freshness, instructions/model behavior, guardrail logic, or human handoff. That avoids treating every poor answer as a prompt problem.
