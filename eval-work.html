Current state

Designed, not yet presented as production evidence. Classification and retrieval cases are developed enough to inspect. Draft quality, appropriate human escalation, and confidence/review routing remain in progress. Offline evals test controlled behavior; they do not prove customer or business outcomes.

Five evaluation areas

1 · Ticket classification

Straightforward, ambiguous, customer-mislabeled, and multi-issue tickets. Wrong routing is scored by its operational consequence.

2 · Knowledge retrieval

Correct-source, no-source, wrong-source temptation, and stale/conflicting-source behavior. The system must not be graded on authority information it was never given.

3 · Draft quality

Grounding, factual and policy accuracy, completeness, tone, and whether the response answers the actual question.

4 · Appropriate escalation

Financial, security, and account-change cases are included to verify recognition, no-draft behavior, and human routing—not draft quality.

5 · Confidence and review routing

Ambiguity, missing information, conflicting evidence, and safe fallback. Confidence must be tied to observable evidence rather than model self-assurance.

Failure diagnosis

Failures should be classified before fixing them: taxonomy/routing, retrieval, source authority or freshness, instructions/model behavior, guardrail logic, or human handoff. That avoids treating every poor answer as a prompt problem.
