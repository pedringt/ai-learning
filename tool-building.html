AI-Assisted Tool Building

Turning real needs into usable tools through structured requirements, rapid prototyping, iterative QA, and human judgment. Unlike Meridian and Harborstone, these are tools I actually built and use.

Why this case exists

What the simulations can't show

Meridian and Harborstone demonstrate organizational AI judgment against fictional scenarios. This case adds something different: what actually happens when I use AI to build something real, from an ambiguous personal need through to a working, shipped artifact — including the parts that went wrong before they went right.

Working tool

Family Food File

A low-decision weekly meal planner for the family — one shared food file, practical grocery list, and a set of "Paige" boosts for portioning and pairing. Built and used for real weekly planning, not a demo.

What I practiced: turning unstructured food preferences into reusable context, distinguishing firm exclusions from flexible ones, and reconciling different household needs.

Family Activity Picker

A kid-outing recommendation tool originally built from a real saved-places list — weather-aware, drive-time-aware, and scored against practical constraints like stroller access and indoor/outdoor fit. The public version uses replacement locations so the real family dataset stays private while the functionality remains testable.

The AI Learning Portfolio Itself

The portfolio you're reading is also an applied AI-assisted project. AI accelerated research, structure, implementation, and iteration; I retained responsibility for requirements, content decisions, visual review, testing, privacy review, and final acceptance of every change.

What I practiced: audience definition, information architecture, cross-page consistency, mobile/visual QA, and applying external feedback selectively rather than automatically.

Public-demo boundary

These are real tools, but their public versions are intentionally sanitized. Children’s names and real family locations were removed or replaced, and personal details were limited to what was necessary to demonstrate how the tools work.

Working approach

How I worked with AI

These tools were built through an iterative collaboration rather than a single generated result. I provided the real needs, constraints, preferences, and acceptance criteria; AI helped translate them into working interfaces, recommendation logic, and successive revisions.

The AI also contributed to quality control by identifying and correcting some implementation, privacy, and usability problems without waiting for me to specify every fix. My role was to evaluate those findings, decide which changes fit the actual goal, test whether the result worked for its intended use, and make the final publishing decisions.

Context

Define the real need

Describe the people, constraints, recurring decisions, and practical tradeoffs the tool needs to support.

Collaboration

Give AI room to contribute

Use AI for implementation, iteration, and problem detection—not only for executing narrowly prescribed changes.

Ownership

Make the final call

Evaluate recommendations, verify that the result is useful and safe, and decide what is acceptable to publish.
