Building State with AI

I built State to test whether a project’s current understanding could be maintained outside the AI instead of repeatedly reconstructed from its history. Building and using it showed me how much control a product needs around AI interpretation.

The challenge

How do you let AI interpret messy project information without quietly deciding what becomes true?

As AI projects got longer, I kept carrying summaries from one chat to the next. I wanted to know whether maintaining a project summary would work better than reconstructing context from history.

I compared raw history, stronger reconstruction instructions, and a maintained summary. The maintained version performed slightly better, but the real finding came from a failure: AI repeatedly turned an unresolved automation target into a confident 0%. Unknown had become none.

What changed for me

The problem was no longer just preserving context.

I realized I didn't want important project truth to depend on what an AI remembered or reconstructed at all.

I needed an external record that both people and AI could work from, while keeping accepted understanding, new evidence, unresolved information, and project history separate.

I kept going because I wanted to understand a harder question: what should AI be allowed to change, and what should stay under human control?

What I built

New information stays separate from accepted Current State.

I built the rule-based foundation, then turned it into a product I could test. Six parts keep new information separate from what the team has accepted:

NotesWhat came in

Meeting notes, decisions, corrections, and approved Slack conversations are preserved as evidence.

Current StateWhat the team relies on now

A readable view presents what the project currently treats as true.

ReviewWhat may need to change

Recommendations wait for a human to accept or leave unchanged.

Open ItemsWhat still needs attention

Reviews, blockers, and open questions stay visible without being mistaken for facts.

HistoryHow the understanding changed

Accepted changes keep before, after, reason, decision, and source together.

AskHow the context gets used

Answers keep accepted facts, pending reviews, questions, and evidence distinct.

AI interprets→software enforces→people decide

Only approved Slack channels are evaluated automatically into Evidence. Everything else in Notes is still entered by hand.

The product decisions

The most useful lessons came from what failed or was harder to use than I expected.

UncertaintyNot all project information should have the same authority.

Evidence can be useful without becoming Current State. Questions stay unresolved until there is enough information to resolve them, and “no change” is a valid Review outcome. That separation mattered more once AI started interpreting the information.

Human controlStructured AI output can still be wrong.

A model response can match the expected format and still point to the wrong fact, an outdated change, or something reasonable but not useful. Software checks references and versions before anything reaches Review. AI can propose a change, but it cannot update Current State on its own.

Product designCorrect data structures still need a usable product.

The first Current State view exposed separate facts but felt like a database viewer, so I kept the structure and redesigned the presentation into something readable. Ask taught a similar lesson: it once returned the wrong billing contact because of retrieval routing, not the model. Fixing the route fixed the answer.

Where I landed

State is most useful when a team needs more than a plausible summary.

State is not necessary for every project. A maintained Claude Artifact or similar document is probably easier and good enough for lightweight solo work. State becomes more useful when several people contribute information, changes are consequential, projects run long enough that history becomes hard to reconstruct, or the team needs to keep new evidence and unresolved questions separate from what it has actually accepted as current.

My biggest takeaway was that AI interpretation and product authority need to be separate, and uncertainty needs to remain visible instead of being smoothed into a plausible answer.

How I worked with AI
My role

Product concept, requirements, UX direction, evaluation, acceptance criteria, tradeoffs, hands-on use, and final decisions.

AI’s role

Technical approaches, much of the frontend/backend implementation, model integration, debugging, log analysis, and automated QA.

I used ChatGPT and Claude Code as collaborators throughout the build, getting more deliberate about handing off work without losing important decisions or constraints.
