Building State with AI

I built State to test whether a project’s current understanding could be maintained instead of reconstructed from its history every time. Building and using it became a way to test where AI interpretation helps, where software rules matter, and where people still need final authority.

The challenge

How do you let AI interpret messy project information without quietly deciding what becomes true?

I started with a simpler problem: as AI projects got longer, I kept carrying summaries from one chat to the next and wanted to know whether maintaining a project summary would work better than reconstructing context from history.

I compared raw history, stronger reconstruction instructions, and a maintained summary. The maintained version performed slightly better, but the more important finding came from a failure: AI repeatedly turned an unresolved automation target into a confident 0%. “Unknown” had become “none.”

What changed for me

The problem was no longer just preserving more context.

I needed to keep accepted understanding, new evidence, unresolved information, and project history meaningfully separate.

A maintained Claude Artifact would likely be enough for lightweight solo work. I kept going because the harder question—what AI should and should not be allowed to change—was the part I wanted to understand.

What I built

I separated what came in, what was accepted, and what still needed judgment.

I built the deterministic foundation first, then spent about three focused days turning it into an AI-integrated product I could use, break, and refine. The model has a bounded job: interpret messy updates and recommend what they might mean. Software checks the rules around those recommendations, and a person decides whether consequential changes become accepted Current State.

NotesWhat came in

Meeting notes, decisions, corrections, observations, and approved Slack conversations are preserved as evidence.

Project StateWhat the team relies on now

A readable project view presents Current State: the understanding the team has actually accepted.

ReviewWhat may need to change

Consequential recommendations wait for a human to update Current State or leave it unchanged.

Open ItemsWhat still needs attention

Reviews, blockers, and ordinary questions stay visible without being mistaken for facts.

HistoryHow the understanding changed

Accepted changes keep their before, after, reason, decision, and source notes together.

AskHow the context gets used

Answers preserve the difference between accepted facts, pending reviews, blockers, questions, history, and supporting evidence.

New information→AI interprets→Software checks→A human decides→Current State updates

Only approved Slack channels are evaluated automatically into Evidence. Everything else in Notes is still entered by hand.

The product decisions

The useful lessons came from where the system failed or became hard to use.

AuthorityKeep information separated by what it is allowed to mean.

Evidence can be useful without becoming a fact, and “no change” is a valid Review outcome. Open questions stay unresolved instead of being smoothed into an answer. That separation became more important once AI was doing interpretation, not less.

Human controlConsequential changes need software checks and a human decision.

A model response can match the expected structure and still point to the wrong fact, recommend an outdated change, or propose nothing meaningful. State saves the original Note before analysis, then software validates references, versions, and constraints before anything reaches Review. AI can propose a change; it cannot update Current State on its own.

Project StateThe cleanest data structure was not the clearest product experience.

The first version exposed Current State as technically distinct facts. It worked underneath, but felt like a database viewer. I kept the facts separate and redesigned Project State as a readable view of what the team currently treats as true.

AskAI needed to use the context without flattening its authority.

Ask keeps accepted facts, pending Reviews, blockers, questions, history, and evidence visibly different. When State once returned the wrong billing contact, the real bug was retrieval routing the question down the wrong path, not the model; fixing the routing fixed the answer. I also traced long Ask waits to model inference, rejected an early streaming implementation that introduced rendering artifacts, and later added progressive output once it could be done reliably.

Where I landed

State is most useful when a team needs more than a plausible summary.

The maintained view became easier to rely on once the project accumulated decisions, contradictory updates, and unresolved questions. Review and History mattered when I cared about how a change became accepted, not just whether a model could reconstruct a convincing answer from past text.

I would not use State for every project. A maintained Claude Artifact or similar document is probably easier and good enough for lightweight solo work. State becomes more useful when changes are consequential, several people contribute information, or the team needs to distinguish new evidence from the understanding it has actually accepted. It also becomes more useful as a project runs longer and its history gets harder to reconstruct from scratch.

The most useful lesson was learning to separate the model’s job from the product’s rules, preserve uncertainty instead of smoothing it away, and investigate whether a failure came from the AI, retrieval, software, or the product design itself.

How I worked with AI
My role

Product concept, requirements, UX direction, evaluation, acceptance criteria, tradeoffs, hands-on use, and final decisions about what was worth changing.

AI’s role

Technical approaches, much of the frontend/backend implementation, model integration, debugging, log analysis, and automated QA.

I used ChatGPT and Claude Code as collaborators throughout the build, and got more deliberate over time about handing work between them without losing important decisions or constraints.
