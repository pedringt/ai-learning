State

Keeping project knowledge current without letting AI decide what’s true.

State turns incoming project notes and Slack messages into proposed changes and questions for human review, helping maintain a current project record.

My role Product framing, requirements, UX direction, product decisions, evaluation, QA & release reviewAI’s role All of the code, plus technical research and engineering recommendations (ChatGPT and Claude)

Status: Working prototype with a shared example project · not yet used by a team.

The problem

Project context was scattered. Keeping a trustworthy current state was harder.

Working with AI let me move through projects much faster, but as chats grew longer, I kept having to remind it of things we'd already discussed. I also started noticing details it had left out or gotten wrong. That made me wonder whether the kind of project notes and documentation I kept as a project manager would hold up if AI maintained them instead.

Using notes from simulated exercises in Claude, I tested ways of carrying context between chats: raw history, stronger reconstruction instructions, and a maintained summary. The maintained summary worked best, but it could still contain conclusions the notes didn't support. The 0% case was the worst offender: the team had not decided what percentage of support tickets to target for automation, and AI recorded 0%, making an unresolved question look like a decision to automate none. The question shifted from “how do we keep context?” to “who decides what counts as true?”

What I built

State maintains a current, trustworthy, human-approved understanding of a project as new information arrives.

Something happens→Evidence→AI interprets→Human decides→State records the outcome

Evidence is whatever comes in: a note added by hand, an uploaded document, or a message from an approved Slack channel. State keeps it unchanged. AI compares it with the project's Current State (what the project treats as true now), the Reviews still waiting on a decision, and the open Questions, then recommends what it means:

  • Genuinely new information, such as a decision the record doesn't have yet, becomes a proposed new Current State item.
  • Changed information becomes a proposed update to, or retirement of, an existing item.
  • Conflicting information that makes an existing item uncertain, without settling what replaces it, becomes a Review flagging that item as at risk. A person can keep tracking the risk as a Question.
  • An unknown worth tracking becomes a suggested Question. It is only created if a person approves it.
  • Nothing consequential: the Evidence stays on record and available to Ask, with no Review and no change to Current State. A person can still ask State to reconsider it.

Not every detail needs its own decision. Related changes from the same Evidence are grouped into one Review, an open Review is reused when the same decision is already pending, and exact duplicates of what Current State already says are dropped.

In Review, a person decides: update Current State, adjust the wording first, leave it unchanged, create or link a Question, or dismiss. Software enforces the rules around that decision. It checks the AI's output before anyone sees it, blocks an approval if the item changed since the proposal was made, and applies each decision completely or not at all. Nothing reaches Current State without a person's decision.

When an approved change alters Current State, History records the before, the after, and the Evidence behind it. Decisions that change nothing, such as leaving an item as it is or creating a Question, add no History. Ask reads across all of it but cannot change anything, and its answers keep accepted facts separate from pending Reviews and open Questions. Each Ask request is answered from the project context. To refine an answer, edit the question and ask again; Ask does not retain a conversational thread.

A new project starts in Baseline Setup, where a person confirms routine starting facts together, while conflicts and open choices still get their own Review. After that, updates follow the ongoing loop above.

Why not just use a Claude Project?

A Claude Project could probably handle my immediate personal need by keeping the source material and instructions together. I kept going because State let me explore a different problem: maintaining an explicit current understanding as new evidence arrives, while keeping the decision about what becomes authoritative outside the model.

The key product decisions

AI can interpret what changed. It cannot decide what becomes true.

The product problem was authority: interpretation needed to move quickly, but project truth could not depend on a model sounding confident. I drew the boundary so AI handles messy interpretation, software enforces the rules, and people authorize consequential changes.

Review workload was the tradeoff. Human review can become its own failure mode: if every detail needed a decision, review would add work without adding control. State first filters for consequential changes, then separates only the decisions that need independent judgment.

Testing changed how I evaluated it.

One eval expected an answer-only outcome, but the evidence justified an additional proposed update. That update met State's product rules, so I revised the expectation because the behavior was correct, not to get a higher pass rate. In earlier testing, an Ask path took roughly 35–38 seconds and longer notes 40–60 seconds to interpret; later, ordinary Evidence submissions were closer to 14 seconds. Those weren't controlled before-and-after measurements, but they pushed me to measure where time went, trim unnecessary context, and treat waiting as a product-design problem.

Lingering questions

What real-team use would still need to show

Review burdenHow does review burden scale?

State filters for consequential changes and groups them into decision-sized Reviews. Only use by a larger team would show whether that workload stays manageable.

Source completenessDoes State have enough of the project to stay trustworthy?

Whether the sources I approved capture the decisions and corrections someone would actually go looking for later.

Where I landed

State may help when a team needs more than a plausible summary.

It fits projects where context changes often and earlier decisions may need revising without losing track of what currently counts as true. Whether it holds up for a real team depends on the two open questions above: manageable review and complete enough sources.
