Building State with AI

I built State to test whether a project’s current understanding could be maintained instead of reconstructed from its history every time. Building and using it helped me see where AI interpretation was useful, where software rules were needed, and where people still needed final authority.

The challenge

How do you let AI interpret messy project information without quietly deciding what becomes true?

I started with a simpler problem. As AI projects got longer, I kept carrying summaries from one chat to the next. I wanted to know whether maintaining a project summary would work better than reconstructing context from history.

I compared raw history, stronger reconstruction instructions, and a maintained summary. The maintained version performed slightly better, but the more important finding came from a failure. AI repeatedly turned an unresolved automation target into a confident 0%. “Unknown” had become “none.”

What changed for me

The problem was no longer just preserving more context.

I needed to keep accepted understanding, new evidence, unresolved information, and project history separate.

A maintained Claude Artifact would likely be enough for lightweight solo work. I kept going because I wanted to understand the harder question: what should AI be allowed to change, and what should stay under human control?

What I built

I separated what came in, what was accepted, and what still needed judgment.

I built the rule-based foundation first, then spent about three focused days turning it into an AI-integrated product I could use, break, and refine. The model has a limited job: interpret messy updates and recommend what they might mean. Software checks those recommendations against the product rules. A person decides whether important changes become accepted Current State.

NotesWhat came in

Meeting notes, decisions, corrections, observations, and approved Slack conversations are preserved as evidence.

Project StateWhat the team relies on now

A readable project view presents Current State: the understanding the team has actually accepted.

ReviewWhat may need to change

Important recommendations wait for a human to update Current State or leave it unchanged.

Open ItemsWhat still needs attention

Reviews, blockers, and ordinary questions stay visible without being mistaken for facts.

HistoryHow the understanding changed

Accepted changes keep their before, after, reason, decision, and source notes together.

AskHow the context gets used

Answers keep accepted facts, pending reviews, blockers, questions, history, and supporting evidence distinct.

New information→AI interprets→Software checks→A human decides→Current State updates

Only approved Slack channels are evaluated automatically into Evidence. Everything else in Notes is still entered by hand.

The product decisions

The most useful lessons came from things that failed or were harder to use than I expected.

AuthorityNot all project information should have the same authority.

Evidence can be useful without becoming a fact, and “no change” is a valid Review outcome. Open questions stay unresolved instead of being turned into an answer. That separation became more important once AI was interpreting the information.

Human controlImportant changes need software checks and a human decision.

A model response can match the expected structure and still point to the wrong fact, recommend an outdated change, or propose nothing useful. State saves the original Note before analysis. Software then checks references, versions, and other constraints before anything reaches Review. AI can propose a change, but it cannot update Current State on its own.

Project StateA clean data structure did not automatically make a clear product.

The first version exposed Current State as separate facts. It worked underneath, but felt like a database viewer. I kept the facts separate and redesigned Project State as a readable view of what the team currently treats as true.

AskAsk needed to use project context without treating everything as equally reliable.

Ask keeps accepted facts, pending Reviews, blockers, questions, history, and evidence visibly different. When State once returned the wrong billing contact, I traced the problem to retrieval sending the question down the wrong path rather than the model itself. Fixing the routing fixed the answer. I also traced long Ask waits to model inference, rejected an early streaming version that caused rendering problems, and later added progressive output once it worked reliably.

Where I landed

State is most useful when a team needs more than a plausible summary.

The maintained view became easier to rely on once the project accumulated decisions, contradictory updates, and unresolved questions. Review and History mattered when I cared about how a change became accepted, not just whether a model could reconstruct a convincing answer from past text.

I would not use State for every project. A maintained Claude Artifact or similar document is probably easier and good enough for lightweight solo work. State becomes more useful when changes matter, several people contribute information, or the team needs to distinguish new evidence from the understanding it has actually accepted. It also becomes more useful as a project runs longer and its history gets harder to reconstruct from scratch.

My biggest takeaway was that the model’s job and the product’s rules need to be separate. The product also needs to preserve uncertainty instead of smoothing it away. When something goes wrong, I need to figure out whether the problem came from the model, retrieval, software, or the product design itself.

How I worked with AI
My role

Product concept, requirements, UX direction, evaluation, acceptance criteria, tradeoffs, hands-on use, and final decisions about what was worth changing.

AI’s role

Technical approaches, much of the frontend/backend implementation, model integration, debugging, log analysis, and automated QA.

I used ChatGPT and Claude Code as collaborators throughout the build. Over time I got more deliberate about handing work between them without losing important decisions or constraints.
