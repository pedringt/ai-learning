Measurement principle

The pilot keeps mandatory human review, so it should not be judged as though it were already autonomous. I separate workflow adoption, AI quality, human intervention, customer and operational outcomes, and economics so one healthy metric cannot hide a failing layer.

Targets and evidence

Why these targets changed

AHT and first response answer different questions

Mandatory review limits near-term handle-time savings, while pre-drafting may still reduce how long eligible tickets wait in the queue. I therefore measure them separately instead of letting one proxy for the other.

The estimate moved from about six to seven hours

The earlier estimate assumed quick acknowledgement was already a distinct workflow. Discovery suggested only two of eight reps used it occasionally, so I revised the target to a more modest blended improvement rather than defend a fragile baseline.

Cost remains a decision constraint

I would not claim a pilot cost outcome until eligible volume, net time saved after review and rework, tool or inference cost, and maintenance burden are known. Cost still shapes the scale decision; it is simply not yet a defensible target.

Reflection

The stronger answer was often “I do not know yet; here is the evidence or input needed,” not a confident guess. Concrete workflow examples and pushback helped me separate adjacent measures and make the unknowns actionable.

What changed in my thinking

  • I rejected blanket daily use as an adoption target.
  • I removed fragile manual workflow tagging when existing timestamps could answer the question.
  • I reduced the expected response-time improvement after discovery challenged the baseline.
  • I stopped promising meaningful pilot AHT reduction while every draft still requires fact-checking.

Decision at the end

The review should end with a scale, change, pause, or stop recommendation—not merely a dashboard. Selective automation would be considered only for low-risk, well-supported ticket types that earn it through eval and pilot evidence.
